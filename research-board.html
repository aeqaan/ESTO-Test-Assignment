Problem

I couldn't find a free reminder before the due date — the only one I found is SMS in Premium (€1.99/mo). When money is short, the help ESTO already has sits in Invoices and Agreements, and the customer has to find it (UX audit hypothesis).

Idea

A free reminder 3 days before and on the due date, by e-mail. Each one carries “Pay” and a help block that opens a short flow starting with one question.

Why ESTO cares

An overdue invoice blocks credit line withdrawals (interface copy), so the customer takes no new money from the line and creates no new interest on it (inference). Interest is ≈80% of revenue (our calc., 9M 2026). CCD2 requires forbearance before collection: Latvia and Lithuania have adopted national laws; Estonia's is planned from 20.05.2027, if adopted.

Main metric

Share of invoices paid on time — A/B vs control in two steps, pilot in Estonia; target +5 pp (my estimate for the test design).

Comparing the laws (inference)

1. One direction: BNPL becomes credit with a customer check.

2. The jump for ESTO is probably smaller than for humm: ESTO already checks creditworthiness for ESTO 3 and Pay Later.

3. The main load is new screens and customer rights, not new fields.

4. Rules already apply in LV and LT; Estonia (≈70% of the portfolio) — planned from 20.05.2027.

5. Lesson from humm (hypothesis, H39): don't move everyone through a new application; take data from the bank or the tax office; measure the funnel step by step.

Key insights (desk research; no interviews with customers or merchants)

All insights come from desk research: reports, websites, help centers, the customer portal (an empty account + UI copy) and research papers. No interviews with ESTO customers or merchants were done. Highlighted insights are the ones the chosen concept rests on.

1. ESTO is an interest lender; BNPL is the funnel. Interest is 80% of revenue in the KPI table (our calc.: €24.4M / €30.5M, 9M 2026); 87.6% of the P&L income lines (our calc.). CEO (2023): “ESTO uses BNPL as a customer acquisition tool”

2. Growth is slowing, profitability is rising. Revenue y/y: +46% (2023) → +29% (2024) → +14% (2025); 9M 2026: +18% vs 9M 2025 — growth picked up slightly again. Profit 2025 — €10.1M (+48%)

3. The priority is monetizing what's been built. “Not a transition year and not an expansion year” (annual report 2025); 2H 2026 — “recurring credit line revenue” (1H 2026 press release)

4. The network grows, GMV per point of sale falls. €36.0k (2023) → €28.5k (2024) → €21.2k (2025); −15% in 9M 2026 vs 9M 2025 while points of sale grew 49% (our calc.). Three periods in a row; no data for 2022. Part of the drop is probably new points that haven't ramped up yet (inference). ESTO doesn't define “point of sale”

5. Losses fall relative to loans issued: debt sale / write-off losses to loans issued 5.7% (2024) → 3.4% (2025) → 2.9% (9M 2026) (our calc.); in absolute terms +15% in 9M 2026. Caveat: a period's losses also come from earlier loans, so the ratio is approximate (inference)

6. Regulation changed on 20.11.2026 (CCD2): BNPL is consumer credit; forbearance before collection is mandatory. Latvia and Lithuania adopted national laws; Lithuania adds early detection of difficulties (secondary sources). Estonia hasn't adopted its law yet: draft 947 SE, planned from 20.05.2027, if adopted

7. Global BNPL leaders added “light” schedule tools; in the Baltics they're rare. Move one payment: Klarna (up to 7 days, free once per purchase; plus a paid Snooze), Afterpay (up to 7 days, once per order), Zip (first one a month free, then $2), Sezzle (up to 2 weeks), Alma (“at any time”; terms not published), Tabby (paid). Fixed preferred day: Afterpay “Preferred payment day” (by default), Revolut “Change day”, LHV (5/10/15/20, by e-mail). Free reminders: Affirm, Afterpay, Tabby; in the Baltics — Inbank (10 and 3 days before and on the due date) and Artea (push). Limit for discipline: Afterpay, Zip. None of the 4 Baltic competitors lets you move a payment by a few days. ESTO has the “heavy” tools (payment holiday of 1–3 months, lower payment by moving hire purchase to the credit line, autopay — per interface copy) but not the light ones: one date (the 15th); no free reminder before the due date found — SMS reminders only in Premium (€1.99/mo)

8. None of the 20 BNPL providers publicly offers a comparison of a point of sale with similar stores. Recommendations — Afterpay iQ (Next Best Actions, 2021); comparison — Shopify (2023), Stripe (2024); basic analytics — Klarna, Zip, Alma (30+ KPIs); reasons for non-payment — Artea. Merchant portals of Inbank, Klix, LHV, Affirm, Tabby and ESTO not seen from inside

9. BNPL moves to a card and an app (inference from examples): Klarna Card; Affirm Card — 23% of Affirm's GMV (FQ1'27); Afterpay on Cash App Card; Revolut Pay Later. ESTO has neither: credit line money can only be withdrawn to a bank account (free in 1–3 business days, instant — paid)

10. Most people in Estonia say they do everyday shopping mostly in stores: 87% of respondents to the Eesti Pank survey (September 2025, 1,010 people); a third are ready to make large purchases online. Shares of people, not purchases; instalments weren't asked about; ESTO's share of offline applications isn't disclosed

11. How easily customers find it isn't checked (insight 13)

12. Hiring context (not a market insight): the job post names three problems — speed from problem to production, quality across three countries, monetization proven with numbers

13. The portal is richer than the website suggests, but help seems hidden and offered late. Payment holiday, lower payment, autopay, a personal limit estimate and business instalments exist; help sits in Invoices / Agreements and is offered once the customer is already in the portal (UX audit hypothesis; based on an empty account and interface copy)

14. Trust is the portal's weak spot: confirmshaming in opt-outs (“Pass, I don't want to save money”), “INSTANT payouts” vs 1–3 days free, many fee amounts not shown in advance, newsletter and Premium offered in 4 places (based on an empty account and interface copy; opt-out screens not seen)

15. Wherever terms are published, flexibility comes with safeguards: one move per order (Klarna, Afterpay, Zip), not for new customers (Afterpay: first purchase < 42 days), new purchases blocked until paid (Zip, Sezzle), a yearly limit (Afterpay). Alma's terms not found

16. Reminders have the strongest evidence found for paying on time: NBER w17020 (microlender, Uganda) — in the test groups (SMS reminders and a reward for paying on time) the probability of paying on time rose 7–9% (percent, not points; one figure for all test groups); SMS had the same average effect as a reward of 25% of the interest. Karlan et al. (2016) — reminders raise savings. Limits: the evidence is about SMS, microcredit and savings — not e-mail, not help inside a reminder; see insight 21. Transfer to ESTO — inference

17. ESTO earns more per euro of GMV than BNPL providers: ≈17.7% vs 2.3–11.4% (our calc.). A rough comparison: ESTO doesn't define GMV; its revenue includes credit line and loan interest (not merchant GMV); periods differ. With insight 1: keeping a customer in interest products is worth more to ESTO than to a BNPL player (inference)

18. Estonia shrinks, growth is in Latvia and Lithuania from a small base. Non-bank consumer credit in Estonia −7% in 2025 (FSA). ESTO portfolio at end-2025 (CFO letter): Estonia €71M, Latvia €13M (+85%), Lithuania €11M (+109%); at 30.09.2026, 70% of the portfolio is in Estonia

19. I couldn't find a free reminder before the due date at ESTO; the only one I found is paid. SMS payment reminders are a Premium feature. The invoice comes free by e-mail (issued on the 1st, due on the 15th). After the due date: “we send you reminders, and some of them are also chargeable” — amount not disclosed (help center, checked 23.11.2026)

20. An unpaid invoice cuts the customer off from the credit line: withdrawals are blocked (whether an issued or only an overdue invoice counts isn't clear from interface copy); after past late payments the account is “temporarily suspended” → a re-check with a one-off fee (per interface copy); after 45 days ESTO may report the debt to the default register. With insight 1: while withdrawals are blocked, the customer takes no new money from the line and creates no new interest on it (inference); the effect on new instalment purchases isn't checked

21. A reminder without a way out can move the debt elsewhere. Medina (Review of Financial Studies, 2021; Brazil): reminders about a credit card payment cut card late fees by 14%, but overdraft fees rose 9% — among users with an overdraft history. All fees combined: +5% for them, −15% for everyone else

Hypotheses: H38, H10, H40, H11, H13, H17, H20, H15, H31

Team: Personal; credit line, hire purchase, ESTO 3 / Pay Later

Why strong: the strongest evidence found (NBER, Karlan, Medina — on SMS, savings and cards); timely (CCD2); builds on what ESTO already has (I3); if Estonia's draft law passes, the first e-mail is required anyway (I19); a paid reminder model filters out the people who need it most (self-selection, H10); a reminder keeps the customer in interest products (I21, H40)

Chosen concept: problem, principles and business logic (H40)

Problem (facts — I20, I21): I couldn't find a free reminder before the due date; the only one I found is paid (SMS in Premium, €1.99/mo). The invoice comes free by e-mail (issued on the 1st, due on the 15th); after the due date some reminders are chargeable. While an invoice is unpaid, credit line withdrawals are blocked.

Design principles

1. Every reminder has a way out. “Pay” plus a help block: “Can't pay by 15 December? You can take a payment holiday, lower your monthly payment or ask for a new date. [See options]”. Only the options the customer's agreement allows are listed. The way out is what ESTO already has: payment holiday, lower payment, a new date on request (ESTO sets it). Reminders help people who forgot; for someone without money, a reminder without a way out is just pressure (inference). Medina (2021): card late fees −14%, but users with an overdraft history paid 5% more in total fees (I24).

2. The reminder before the due date is free for everyone without Premium (H10). Self-selection hypothesis: a subscription is bought upfront mostly by organised people — nobody plans to forget. Check: the A/B effect in the “had late payments” group. I found no payment reminders in the competitor subscriptions studied (I22).

3. E-mail first, web push optional, SMS stays in Premium. ESTO has no mobile app, so push means web push (on iPhone only from the Home Screen, iOS 16.4+). NBER tested SMS (+7–9% across the test groups); there's no such evidence for e-mail → A/B in two steps: reminder × help (2×2, e-mail), then the channel.

4. Every fee as an amount before confirming (H20): the terms of each option are visible before the button; no Premium upsell in these e-mails.

5. No pushing credit in a hard month. “Not for a while” → our team, not a lower payment: moving hire purchase to the credit line is irreversible and the representative rate is higher (11.90% → 37.10%) — the same risk for which direction E scored low. Lower payment stays an option, with full terms.

10 · Chosen concept: a reminder with a way out

Help comes to the customer inside the reminder instead of the customer having to find it in the portal.

A free reminder before the due date, with help inside it — no searching through the portal: the e-mail links straight to the right invoice after Smart-ID / Mobile-ID login (planned).

E-mail first — free for the customer, and ESTO already sends the invoice by e-mail. Web push optional: I found no ESTO mobile app, so push would mean web push (inference) — only for customers who allowed browser notifications, and on iPhone only from the Home Screen (iOS 16.4+, Apple platform limit). SMS stays in Premium. Open the prototype and write-up →

Three customer groups (my model; shares not disclosed)

1. Has money, forgot — misses the date → on the credit line, withdrawals are blocked; may pay for a reminder after the due date (some are chargeable); pays a few days later. Gets: a free reminder 3 days before and on the due date + “Pay” in 1 click.

2. Has money, organised — pays on time (autopay or Premium SMS). Gets: almost nothing new — the group where we check for harm (a segment, not the A/B control).

3. Short of money this month — help exists but sits in Invoices / Agreements, so they have to find it (UX audit hypothesis); otherwise an e-mail to the debt team. Gets: a help block in every e-mail → a wizard with options and terms as amounts.

Chronic inability to pay is for the debt team and risk scoring — a reminder won't solve it (inference); that's why “Not for a while” leads to our team.

Groups can't be labelled in advance. Proxy in ESTO data: a late payment fully closed 1–5 days after the 15th ≈ “forgot”; long or partial ≈ “no money”.

Touchpoints

1. The 1st — invoice by e-mail (already sent free today): amount, due date, “Pay” + help block

2. 3 days before the 15th — e-mail (+ web push if allowed); SMS stays in Premium

3. The 15th — e-mail (+ web push if allowed)

Help flow: one question, “When could you pay?” → by 15 December → pay; in a few days → ask for a new date (ESTO sets it; until then the due date stays); next month → payment holiday; not for a while → talk to our team (lower payment stays in the list with full terms). Before the button, lower payment shows that it can't be undone, that the credit line rate applies and that the total can go up. The checkbox isn't pre-ticked; the button names the action. Test design: step 1 — reminder × help, 2×2, e-mail only: control / help block in the invoice only / reminders without help / reminders + help. Step 2 — the channel on the winning setup: e-mail / + web push / SMS. Randomise by customer, not invoice; Premium subscribers are analysed separately. Pilot in Estonia; Latvia and Lithuania next.

Sample size: from the week-1 baseline; e.g. at 80% on time, +5 pp needs ≈900 invoices per group (α 0.05, power 80% — our calc.). 45+ days late is rare → checked at 6–12 months.

Decision rule: ship if the main metric improves significantly and no guardrail crosses its threshold. Only reminders work → ship them, rework the help flow; only help works → keep the help block in the invoice e-mail.

Guardrails: Premium churn, complaints, unsubscribes; debt team queue and response time; customers who got help and still reached 45+ days late; repeat help requests. Thresholds set with risk and the debt team. Fee income isn't a guardrail — its fall is expected and sits in H40.

Business (H40): net revenue per customer in the same cohorts after 6 and 12 months.

Rough business case

The trade-off: part of the income from late and reminder fees (its size isn't disclosed) for more interest income and lower debt losses. Payback is checked in the A/B cohorts on net revenue per customer over 6–12 months.

Why it can pay back: interest is ≈80% of revenue (€24.4M of €30.5M, 9M 2026); while an invoice is unpaid, credit line withdrawals are blocked → the customer takes no new money from the line and creates no new interest. Scale (our calc.): interest ≈ €32.6M a year → +1% ≈ +€0.33M a year. That counts interest from all products; the effect lands mostly on the credit line, whose share isn't disclosed.

The condition: ESTO doesn't disclose its fee income. At Sezzle (interest-free pay-in-4), consumer fees were $131.9M in 2025, ≈29% of revenue (our calc.) — so I can't assume ESTO's fees are small. Step one: get the figure from ESTO's finance team.

Break-even: extra interest − funding cost of the extra lending (≈9.7% a year, our calc.) + lower debt losses > lost fees + lost Premium revenue + sending costs.

Metric: net revenue per customer = interest + fees + Premium − losses − funding and sending costs. Early signals: credit line withdrawals after the due date, blocked accounts, a new agreement within 12 months, share reaching 45+ days late.

Where the effect is strongest: the credit line — directly; hire purchase and loans — only through repeat purchases. Lower losses come mainly from the help flow: “forgot” late payments usually close within days.

Not in the numbers: Premium offered only once the customer is back on schedule (like Sezzle, whose subscription is only for customers with a good payment history); cheaper funding from lower credit risk (hypothesis).
